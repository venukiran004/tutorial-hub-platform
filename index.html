Most technical material teaches you what to type. The gap between that and being trusted with production systems is judgement — knowing why one approach beats another, what breaks at scale, and what you will be asked to defend in review. That gap is what these courses teach.

Standard

What every lesson is held to

01
A mental model before syntax
Diagrams that show real mechanism. If a picture does not improve understanding it is not drawn.
02
Code that resembles work
Runnable, modern, typed where it helps, and drawn from systems rather than textbooks.
03
Three rungs, not one answer
Bad, better and production-grade, with the reasoning between them made explicit.
04
Trade-offs stated honestly
Where there is no single right answer, you get the decision procedure instead of a rule.
05
Practice you must attempt
Solutions stay hidden until you choose to reveal them, because reading a solution is not learning.
06
Interview and production lenses
What the topic sounds like under questioning, and how it behaves at 3am.

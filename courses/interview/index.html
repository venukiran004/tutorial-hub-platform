The questions, answered the way they were actually asked.

Real question sets from GenAI and agentic-systems interviews, one page per experience. Each page keeps every question that transfers to anybody and leaves out the ones about a candidate’s own project, because that answer is theirs. The questions are grouped by theme rather than kept in the order they were asked, so the follow-ups sit beside what they followed up on — and in these interviews the follow-up was usually the real question, removing a tool and asking what you would do without it. Every answer gives the mechanism rather than the definition, and where a claim could be measured it was measured: the KV cache arithmetic, the accuracy a useless model scores on an imbalanced set, threads against processes, and the three rain-water solutions timed against each other.

What is being checked

Five things, in every round

One of these interviewers wrote it down explicitly: every answer was checked for whether it was theoretical or lived. These are the tells they were reading.

01
A mechanism, not a definition
“A cross-encoder is more accurate” is a definition. “The pair does not exist until the query arrives, so nothing can be precomputed” is a mechanism, and every consequence follows from it.
02
A number attached to the claim
Retrieval is 50–150 ms of a multi-second answer. A 7B model’s KV cache is 250 GiB at batch 16. Threads were 0.68× on CPU work. The number is what cannot be bluffed.
03
A trade-off you accepted
Not a technique list — what you gave up and why. Agentic RAG costs two to four times as much; MQA saves 32× and costs quality; threshold tuning trades precision against recall.
04
A diagnosis before a fix
Was the right chunk retrieved, and was it used? Did the drop arrive as a cliff or a slope? The question you ask first is the strongest signal in a debugging answer.
05
“I have not measured that”
Said where it is true. The next question goes one level deeper, and an honest half-answer survives it where a confident invention collapses in front of the person deciding.

Language, taken apart matrix by matrix.

The reference notes, section by section, in their own order: text turned into tokens and tokens into vectors; the classical task catalogue from classification to translation; the transformer derived from a dot product and then traced on real numbers, twice — a decoder-only forward pass and a full encoder–decoder translation where every matrix is printed; the modern stack from GQA and RoPE to MoE and Mamba; multimodal models; and the fuzzy matching that still runs in production. Every formula is carried through on numbers and every program is the reference's own, run here with its output shown — then the practice programs and the interview bank, every answer folded away until you ask.

The progression

Five states, in order

Every module exists to move you from one of these states to the next. The learn track takes you through them; the practice and interview tracks are where you find out whether it held.

01
I can turn text into numbers
A preprocessing pipeline whose every step has a reason, BPE merges computed by hand, TF-IDF worked on a small corpus, and embeddings whose geometry you can check.
02
I can solve the classical task
Classification, NER, topic models, semantic search, summarisation and QA — each with the metric that judges it and the baseline it has to beat.
03
I can derive attention
Scaled dot-product attention from the dot product up, multi-head as a partition, masking and cross-attention, and the parameter count of a block computed exactly.
04
I can trace one end to end
Every matrix in a decoder-only forward pass and in a full encoder–decoder translation, reproduced in NumPy and checked against the reference's own numbers.
05
I can reason about the modern stack
Why GQA exists and what it costs, what RoPE does that sinusoids do not, when MoE pays, where Mamba wins — and an answer for any question in the bank.

The maths a model rests on, worked rather than quoted.

Linear algebra, calculus, probability and inference — the parts that decide whether a model trains, whether a result is real, and whether a number in a report means what it says. Every formula here is evaluated on an actual number, because a formula you have not worked by hand is one you cannot debug.

The progression

Five states, in order

Every level exists to move you from one of these states to the next. If a lesson does not advance you along this line, it was cut.

01
I can read the notation
A matrix, a gradient and a distribution stop being symbols and start being objects you can picture.
02
I can work the number
You evaluate it by hand on a small case, which is the only way to know a library is lying to you.
03
I can derive it
PCA falls out of an eigenproblem and n minus 1 falls out of a bias calculation, rather than being asserted.
04
I know what it assumes
Every test and every model carries assumptions; you can name them and say what breaks when they fail.
05
I can defend the conclusion
You can say what a result licenses, what it does not, and why the obvious reading is wrong.
Curriculum

The full path

Three tracks, taken in any order or in parallel. Mathematics derives the results in six phases, each depending on the one before it: the algebra a model is written in, the calculus that trains it, the probability that describes its uncertainty, and the statistics that decides whether a measured difference is real. Practice puts fifty scenarios in front of you where the mathematics decides the answer, and Interview is a hundred questions with the working folded away.
